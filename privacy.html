Privacy

Effective 23 October 2026 · version 4.0.0

The short version: ATLAS has no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. Your progress never leaves your device. The only network traffic the game generates is (a) loading its own files, and (b) optional peer-to-peer multiplayer, described below.

1. What is stored, and where

Everything the game remembers is written to your browser's localStorage under one key, atlas.profile.v1:

  • your chosen player name and avatar;
  • preferences: sound, haptics, live validation;
  • aggregate statistics: matches, wins, points, best score, best streak, best chain, energy earned and spent, power-ups fired;
  • per-category best scores; and
  • your thirty most recent match results (score, place, category, mode).

This data exists only on the device you played on. There is no server-side profile, no sync, and no way for the operator of this site to read it. Clearing your browser storage (or the "Reset progress" button in the profile screen) deletes it permanently.

2. What is never collected

  • No account, email address, phone number or payment details — the game does not ask for any.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or advertising SDKs are bundled.
  • No third-party cookies. The game sets no cookies at all.
  • No location, contacts, camera, microphone or microphone-adjacent permissions. The only optional device APIs used are vibration (haptics) and screen wake-lock during a match, both of which you can switch off or which fail silently if denied.

3. Multiplayer: what travels, and to whom

When you create or join an online room, the game opens a peer-to-peer connection (WebRTC) between the devices in that room. The host device holds the match; every other device sends its moves to the host and receives match states back.

What leaves your device in a room: your player name, and the moves you make (words submitted, passes, power-ups). These go only to the other devices in your room, over an encrypted WebRTC channel.

One exception: establishing the connection requires a brief handshake through a public signalling server operated by the PeerJS project. That handshake carries the room code and connection identifiers — not your words, not your statistics, not your name. Game traffic never transits that server.

When the room closes, the match state is discarded by every device. Nothing is retained by any server, because no server holds it.

4. Offline caching

The game installs a service worker so it opens instantly and works without a connection. It caches the game's own files (code, styles, fonts, icons, photography) in your browser's cache storage. That cache contains no personal data and is removed when you uninstall the app or clear site data.

5. Children

The game is suitable for all ages and, because it collects nothing, imposes no age gate. If you deploy a modified copy with accounts, analytics or chat, you — not this project — become responsible for complying with children's privacy law such as COPPA and the GDPR-K provisions.

6. Your rights

Because no personal data is processed by any server, there is no controller holding records about you to exercise access, rectification or erasure rights against. Your copy of your data is on your device and you can delete it at any time. If you believe this statement is inaccurate for a deployment you are using, contact that deployment's operator.

7. If you run your own copy

This page describes the unmodified game. If you add analytics, accounts, payments or advertising to a fork, you must write your own privacy notice and replace this page. Shipping a modified build with this page intact while collecting data would misrepresent your product.

8. Changes and contact

This notice is versioned with the game (see the effective date above). Questions or concerns: open an issue in the project repository.
